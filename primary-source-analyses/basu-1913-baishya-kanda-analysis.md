## Key Negative Finding: De/Dey Family NOT Classified as Vaishya

The Baishya Kanda covers Bengal's Vaishya (merchant) communities comprehensively. The De/Dey family is **not mentioned anywhere** in this volume as Vaishya. This confirms that:

1. **Dey/De/Deb = Kayastha**, not Vaishya
2. The question "are the Deys Kayastha or Vaishya?" is answered definitively: **KAYASTHA**
3. There is no historical tradition of classifying the De/Dey surname as Vaishya

## What the Baishya Kanda DOES Cover

### Vaishya Communities in Bengal
The principal Vaishya jati groups covered are:
- **Souluk-Saha** (also: Sauluk, Saoluk) — the largest merchant community
- **Barendra Saha** — sub-group, in Mymensingh / North Bengal
- **Gandha Banik** — spice merchants
- **Shankha Banik** — conch traders
- **Kamsa Banik** — bell-metal traders
- **Suvarna Banik** — gold traders (e.g., Lal Behari Dey was this caste)

### Souluk/Saha Origin Story
- Souluk = descended from the Chalukya dynasty (Solanki Rajputs of Gujarat)
- "Saulak" → "Souluk" in East Bengal
- Confirmed as proper Vaishya (Dvija) community
- Lost social status due to **Sen king's guru's curse** (a Brahmin rajguru cursed the Souluk Agrawals)

### Mymensingh Vaishya Families
Line 2809: Mymensingh's Souluk Saha merchants lived along Brahmaputra's north bank, traded widely. Key families: Barikaandi Chowdhury family, Kabuliyabari Chowdhury family.

Line 2471: Barendra Saha distribution: Mymensingh's Tangail subdivision, Dhaka (Manikganj, Narayanganj, Munshiganj), Pabna (Sirajganj), Faridpur.

## Key Distinction: Dey vs Saha in Mymensingh

In Mymensingh:
- **Dey/De** (like Aswini Kumar De) = **Kayastha** — scribal/administrative class, educated professionals
- **Saha** families = **Vaishya/Souluk** — merchant/trading class

These were two distinct social communities. Robin's grandfather (Aswini Kumar De, umbrella manufacturer) was of the **Kayastha** class, not the Vaishya merchant class. His business enterprise was consistent with the Kayastha class's adaptation to commercial activities under colonial rule — not an indication of Vaishya origin.

## Significant Historical Passage: Souluk Saha Demotion

Line 2380:
```
তাহার! প্রকৃত বৈশ্ত ও নিষ্ঠাবান আধ্যসন্তান বলিয়া পরিচিত হইলেও রাজগুরুর অভিশাপে তাহাদের অধোগতি ঘটিয়াছিল।
```
**Translation**: "Although they were known as true Vaishyas and devout Aryan descendants, through the curse of the royal guru their social decline occurred."

This passage about the Souluk Saha families dramatically illustrates how caste status could be formally degraded in the Sen/medieval Bengali system — by contrast, the Dey family maintained their Kayastha status through this period.

## Book Contents Summary

The Baishya Kanda is primarily a **history of world commerce** in the ancient and medieval periods, rather than a caste genealogy book like the Kayastha Kanda. It covers:
1. Ancient Vedic Vaishya origins
2. Indian maritime commerce (Arabia, Southeast Asia, Americas)
3. Arthashastra on trade regulations
4. Rise and fall of Vaishya political power in Bengal
5. Souluk/Saha community genealogies and notable families
