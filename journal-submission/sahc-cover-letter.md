I am pleased to submit for your consideration the article **"Origins, Social Identity, and Documentary History of the De/Dey Family of East Bengal, c. 1200–1947"** for publication in *South Asian History and Culture*.

## Summary of the Paper's Argument

The paper traces the De/Dey surname — one of the most widespread among Bengali Hindus — through five analytical lenses: etymology, medieval epigraphy, Bengali Kayastha caste sociology, population genetics, and micro-historical family study. It argues that the surname crystallised as a hereditary identifier in the period 1231–1317 CE in proximity to the Vaishnava Deva dynasty of Bikrampur and Sonargaon, that Dey families occupy a distinctive dual position in Bengali Kayastha social classification (*Mahapatra* in the Bangaja eastern framework, *Siddha Maulika* in the Dakshin-Radhi western framework), and that this social position can be recovered from an interlocking set of sources — kulagrantha texts, colonial census and ethnographic data, copper-plate inscriptions, published judicial records, and a 2017 population genetics study — whose individual limitations are addressed explicitly and whose convergence provides robust cross-source confirmation.

## Significance and Contribution

This article makes the following contributions to South Asian historical scholarship:

1. **Kulagrantha methodology**: Offers a critically-grounded treatment of the Bengali kulagrantha tradition — synthesising Chatterjee (2005, 2009), Inden (1976), and Bhattasali (1922) — as a model for using genealogical texts responsibly alongside epigraphic and colonial archival evidence.

2. **Epigraphy + social classification**: Demonstrates how the Damodaradeva copper-plate grants (1234–1243 CE) and the Danujamardana tradition (15th c.) can be connected to traceable caste-sociological structures that persist into the modern period.

3. **Interdisciplinary integration**: Integrates population genetics (Pagani et al. 2017) with historical and sociological evidence, while explicitly acknowledging the n=1 sample limitation as a methodological issue requiring further research.

4. **Colonial legal history as genealogical source**: Demonstrates how a single Calcutta High Court judgment (*Loke Nath Sen v. Aswini Kumar Dey*, AIR 1938 Cal 216) can serve as a micro-historical anchor for tracing a specific East Bengali family through the last century of British colonial administration.

5. **East Bengali specificity**: Addresses an underrepresented geography — Mymensingh and the Bakla/Chandradwip territory — in the history of Bengali caste and social organisation, providing a counterpoint to the West Bengal-centred literature.

The paper is, to my knowledge, the first English-language academic study to focus specifically on the De/Dey surname's historical and social formation, and to integrate the full range of kulagrantha, epigraphic, genetic, and archival sources in a single analysis.
