# Response to Peer Review
## *Origins, Social Identity, and Documentary History of the De/Dey Family of East Bengal, c. 1200–1947*

**Manuscript reference**: JAS submission, Vol. LXVII/LXVIII, 2025–2026  
**Author**: Robin Dey

---

We are grateful to the reviewer for an exceptionally thorough and constructive report that has substantially improved the manuscript. The reviewer's central critique — that Basu was treated as an arms-length authority rather than as an ideologically positioned primary source requiring critical handling — is well-taken and has led to a fundamental restructuring of the paper's methodological scaffolding. We address each of the reviewer's specific requirements in turn.

---

## Major Revisions Made

### 1. Reframing Basu Throughout (Reviewer's Primary Requirement)

We have added a substantial new **Section 2: "Source Methodology: Kulagranthas, Basu, and the Critical Use of Genealogical Evidence"** (approximately 1,600 words) that addresses the reviewer's central concern head-on. This section:

- Explains the kulagrantha genre as "ideologically purposive social documents" (Chatterjee 2005), drawing explicitly on Inden's structural analysis of the three-layer kulagrantha and his palaeographic observation that all such manuscripts are assigned to the 18th–19th centuries regardless of internal dating claims (Inden 1976: 27–28)
- Situates Basu explicitly as a Kayastha activist — founder of the Kayastha Sabha (1902), editor of *Kayastha Patrika* — and quotes Inden's own qualification of his reliance on Basu: "although many of his arguments about the origin of the kāyasthas of Bengal cannot be taken too seriously, I have relied heavily on the hundreds of texts which he collected and published"
- Notes the Mukharji critique as reported (while flagging that the specific Mukharji publication has not been independently located and should be tracked to its primary source)
- Proposes an explicit three-category framework for evaluating Basu-derived claims: **Category A** (corroborated by independent sources), **Category B** (consistent with tradition, cross-attested in multiple compilations), and **Category C** (single-source or inference-dependent)

Throughout the revised paper, all Basu-derived claims are now labelled with their category. Language has been changed throughout: "Basu corrects" → "Basu's kulagrantha compilation transmits a tradition according to which"; "Basu records" → "the kulagrantha tradition, as compiled by Basu, holds"; and so forth.

### 2. The Danujamardana Question (Reviewer's Requirement 2)

Section 4.5 has been completely rewritten to present three distinct scholarly positions:

- **Position 1** (dominant numismatic-historiographic): Bhattasali (1922), Sarkar (1985), Majumdar (1960), Banglapedia ("Raja Ganesha") identify the Danujamarddana coins with Raja Ganesha of Bhaturia/Dinajpur — now stated explicitly as the dominant view
- **Position 2** (minority revisionist): Dani and Eaton (1993: 51, 53–55) distinguish two persons but treat the Chandradwip ruler as a descendant of the Sonargaon Deva dynasty, without endorsing the Kayastha-Shandilya-Karnasuvarna genealogical claim
- **Position 3** (kulagrantha tradition, via Basu): The full Chandradwip-Kayastha-Shandilya-Karnasuvarna argument, now presented explicitly as the kulagrantha tradition's account rather than as settled history

The paper now explicitly states which position it follows for the social-historical argument (Position 3 for the internal Bangaja community-organisation history) and why, while acknowledging that this position is specific to the kulagrantha tradition and is not endorsed by the post-1922 numismatic mainstream.

Bhattasali (1922) and Sarkar (1985) have been added to the reference list.

### 3. The Vaishya/Kayastha "Absence" Argument (Reviewer's Requirement 3)

The absolute formulation ("De/Dey/Dev does not appear anywhere in this volume") has been withdrawn. Section 5.2 now reads:

> "An important caveat is necessary here. Risley's *Tribes and Castes of Bengal* (1891: 216) lists 'De' as a surname found — among others — in the Suvarna Banik community. Suvarna Banik is a major caste covered in the *Baishya Kanda*. A categorical claim that the De/Dey cognomen is entirely absent from the *Baishya Kanda* cannot be sustained without a complete and verified Bengali-language text-search of that volume, which lies beyond the scope of this paper. What can be said, with confidence, is that **De/Dey is not established as a primary genealogical lineage surname in Bengal's Vaishya community**..."

This formulation is — as the reviewer suggests — defensible and adequately supports the paper's core argument without the methodologically untenable absolute negative claim.

### 4. Karnasuvarna Dynastic Continuity (Reviewer's Requirements 7 & 8)

Section 4.1 now opens with an explicit statement that the Karnasuvarna-to-Sonargaon dynastic continuity is assessed as **Category C** (single-source), and explains why: no inscription or copperplate documents the Karnasuvarna-Sonargaon connection; the copperplates of Damodaradeva do not mention Karnasuvarna, do not state the dynasty's gotra, and do not record a Karnasena founder; and the Pala and Sena interregna eliminate any presumption of political continuity. The Karnasuvarna claim is now presented as "the community's own account of its origins" rather than as documentary history.

### 5. Kulpradipa "First" Claim (Reviewer's Requirement — Claim 6)

The language "The Deva dynasty is named **first** among the eight Siddha Maulika families" has been softened throughout to: "listed **first in the verse's metrical enumeration**" of the eight families, with an explicit note (citing Inden 1976: 52–55) that "verse-internal ordering in kulagrantha lists is primarily metrical and incantatory rather than strictly hierarchical" and that in some regional traditions, Dutta is conventionally accorded first rank.

### 6. Batu Bhatta Single-Manuscript Dependency (Reviewer's Requirement 6)

Section 4.1 now explicitly flags the single-manuscript dependency of the Batu Bhatta chain: "This genealogical claim must be assessed as Category C — single-source, requiring independent verification." Section 8.1 (Mymensingh case study) also explicitly acknowledges: "This chain of transmission — Kshitindra Deb Roy → Gobindachandra Deb Roy → Basu — is a single-manuscript provenance of the kind Chatterjee (2005) and Inden (1976) caution against treating as independently verified history. The manuscript has not, to this author's knowledge, been deposited in a public collection or examined by a scholar other than Basu."

### 7. Ten-Gotra Schema (Reviewer's Claim 2)

Section 4.4 now explicitly states the ten-gotra schema is assessed as **Category B** — consistent with the broader kulagrantha corpus and cross-attested, but without epigraphic corroboration. The note "No inscription or copperplate of the historical Deva rulers records any gotra" has been added.

### 8. The 14 Samajes Chronological Claim (Reviewer's Claim 3)

Section 4.7 now notes explicitly: "the 14-samaj list is assessed as Category B: the list reflects genuine social-territorial organisation of the Bangaja Kayastha system as it crystallised in the medieval and early modern periods, but the implicit claim that this geography maps directly onto a 5th-to-13th-century Deva *dynastic* organisation overstates the documentary basis." Bandyopadhyay (2004: 18–25) is cited here for the dating of the kulina-maulika system.

### 9. Mahapatra Qualification (Reviewer's Claim 5)

Section 5.4 now includes the explicit qualifier, following Inden (1976: 46): "Mahapatra status in the Bangaja system was awarded to a specific branch of a specific family of a specific gotra — not to all Dey families across the board."

### 10. Volume Dates and Citation Format (Reviewer's Requirement 5)

Section 2.3 ("The Serial Publication Dates of *Banger Jatiya Itihas*") now explicitly addresses the dating issue: the "1937" date for the Kayastha Kanda is flagged as "not confirmed by a title-page image and sits slightly beyond the 1911–1933 publication window recorded in Basu's standard bibliographic entries," with a recommendation to readers requiring precise edition verification to consult physical copies at the Bangiya Sahitya Parishad or National Library of India. The Kayastha Kanda citation now appears as "Basu, N. ([1937])" in the reference list to signal the uncertainty. The anomalous line-number citation format is also noted, with the author's responsibility qualified accordingly.

### 11. Genetic Evidence Limitations (Reviewer's general point)

Section 7.3 now opens with an explicit statement: "the Dey result derives from a **single individual** (n=1). A single Y-haplogroup result cannot be treated as representative of the Dey community." The section expands on why the ten-gotra schema itself predicts that different gotra branches may carry different Y-haplogroups.

### 12. Risley Date Correction

The erroneous "1892" date for Risley has been corrected to **1891** throughout.

### 13. New References Added

The following references have been added:
- Bhattasali, N.K. (1922)
- Chatterjee, K. (2005)
- Chatterjee, K. (2009)
- Sarkar, J. (1985)

Bandyopadhyay (2004) was already in the reference list and is now more substantively engaged in the text.

## Points Not Addressed and Explanation

### Page-Image Scans (Reviewer's Requirement 4)

The reviewer asks for page-image scans of fn. 183, the Kulpradipa verse, the 14-samaj passage, and other cited passages. These cannot be supplied in this version because: (a) the DLI digitised editions are available in OCR/djvu format but page images at journal-submission quality require physical access to the volumes; (b) the physical volumes are held at the Bangiya Sahitya Parishad (Kolkata) and the National Library of India, which were not accessible to the author during the preparation of this manuscript. The author acknowledges this limitation explicitly in the text and in this response. If the editor considers page-image verification essential to publication, the author would welcome the opportunity to obtain and supply scans during the revision process.

### The Specific Mukharji Publication

The Mukharji critique of Basu as "a dedicated Kayastha propagandist" is noted and cited as it appears in secondary sources (Wikipedia's *Nagendranath Basu* entry), with an explicit flag that the primary Mukharji publication has not been independently located. The author will attempt to trace this before final submission.

## Summary

The revised manuscript is substantially different from the submission in its methodological framing. The empirical core — the caste-sociological analysis, the kulagrantha evidence, the genetic data, and the Mymensingh case study — is retained, but it now rests on an explicitly critical scaffolding that distinguishes between corroborated claims, tradition-internal claims, and contested claims. The reviewer's overall thesis — that "the paper is empirically rich" but required critical distance from Basu — has been fully accepted and acted upon.

We believe the revised manuscript meets the benchmarks the reviewer set for acceptance subject to minor revisions: the Danujamarddana section now engages Bhattasali and Eaton; the absolute Vaishya-absence claim has been withdrawn; and Basu is no longer presented as an authoritative corrector but as a Kayastha activist compiler whose textual compilations are used critically.
