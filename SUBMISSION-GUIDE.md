## Is This Paper a Good Fit?

**Yes — strongly.** The JAS Kolkata publishes historical papers on Bengal, Indian social history, epigraphy, and cultural studies. Recent 2025 issues include papers on:
- Colonial Bengal history ("Colonial Rule in Bengal and Occupational Mobility")
- Ethnography and social history ("Bengali Women and Physical Fitness in Colonial Bengal, 1900–1947")

Your paper — on the social history, genealogy, and genetics of a Bengali family with primary sources going back to the 13th century — is **very well aligned** with the journal's scope.

## Submission Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Manuscript

The JAS Kolkata does **not** use an online submission portal (as of May 2026). Submissions are made **by email or post**.

**Required format:**
- **File type**: Microsoft Word (.docx) preferred; PDF acceptable for initial submission
- **Font**: Times New Roman, 12pt
- **Line spacing**: Double-spaced
- **Margins**: 2.5cm on all sides
- **Paper size**: A4
- **Page numbers**: Yes, bottom centre
- **Abstract**: Required — 150–200 words
- **Keywords**: Required — 5–8 keywords after the abstract
- **Word count**: Target 6,000–10,000 words (your paper is ~10,200 — acceptable for a substantial article)
- **References**: Author-date style as used by the journal (Author, Year: page) — already formatted in this submission
- **Bengali script**: Should render correctly in Times New Roman or Arial Unicode — test before sending

**What to include in the submission email:**
1. The manuscript (.docx)
2. A **cover letter** (see template below)
3. Your contact details (email, phone, affiliation/address)

## Notes on the AI Attribution

The Acknowledgements section of the paper includes explicit AI attribution. This is a novel element in academic submissions. A few points to be aware of:

1. **Transparency is good practice**: Major journals are increasingly requiring AI disclosure. Including it proactively is appropriate and ethical.

2. **What was AI-assisted**: Source retrieval, Bengali text analysis, drafting, and citation formatting.

3. **What was NOT AI-generated**: The interpretive scholarly conclusions, the family history connection (Aswini Kumar De), and the overall research framing.

4. **If the editor queries it**: You can explain that the AI system acted as a research assistant in the same way that a research assistant might search archives and prepare summaries, but that all scholarly judgements, conclusions, and the act of authorship rest with you.
